# Privacy Policy

_Last updated: 6 October 2026_

Scivelle is a word processor for novelists, made by Fredy. This policy explains what the app
does with your writing and your data. The short version: your documents stay wherever you save
them, the app has no account and collects nothing, and text leaves your Mac only when you run
the writing assistant with an AI service you chose to connect — and then only to that service.

## What Scivelle stores, and where

- **Your documents** are files on your Mac (or in a cloud folder you keep them in). Scivelle
  reads and writes them when you open and save, and nowhere else.
- **Version history**: a copy of each document at every save, kept in the app's own data folder
  on your Mac so you can restore an earlier version. These copies never leave your Mac.
- **Settings and preferences** (appearance, accent colour, the daily word goal, what the
  assistant reads, the recent documents list) are kept on your Mac.
- **The trial and the licence**: the date of the first launch (also kept in a second small
  file in Library/Application Support, so the trial survives emptying the app's folder) and,
  once bought, the licence key. Both stay on your Mac.
- **AI connections**: keys you paste and the sign-in tokens from "Continue with ChatGPT" or
  "Continue with OpenRouter" are kept in the app's data folder on your Mac, readable by your user
  account only, and sent only to the service they belong to. Disconnecting removes them.

Scivelle has no user accounts, no analytics, no advertising, no crash reporting and no
telemetry. The developer receives no data from the app.

## When text leaves your Mac

**Apple Intelligence.** By default the writing assistant uses Apple's on-device language model.
Your text is processed on your Mac and is not sent anywhere.

**AI services you connect.** If you connect a provider in Settings — OpenAI (ChatGPT), Anthropic
(Claude), Google (Gemini), OpenRouter, or any server you name — then, each time you run an
assistant task, Ask, or a cast tool, Scivelle sends that provider the text the task needs:

- with *Around the caret*, the passage you selected or the paragraph at the caret and about
  1,400 words around it;
- with *The whole book*, your manuscript (up to 80,000 words), your cast notes and your world
  notes.

That text is handled under the provider's own terms and privacy policy, not this one. Scivelle
sends it only when you ask for an answer, only to the provider you chose, and never to the
developer. The assistant pane always says which service is answering.

**Ollama** runs on your Mac; text sent to it does not leave your computer.

**The licence key.** When you activate a licence, Scivelle sends the key and a name for your
Mac (its computer name) to the store that sold it, to check the key and count the activation;
about once a week, when online, it checks again. Nothing else is sent, and nothing about your
documents. Deactivating tells the store to release this Mac. The Mac App Store version has no
keys and makes none of these requests.

**Sign in with ChatGPT.** "Continue with ChatGPT" opens OpenAI's sign-in page in your browser.
Scivelle receives tokens that let it use your ChatGPT plan, and your account's email address to
show you who is signed in. It never sees your password or your ChatGPT conversations. Signing
out revokes the tokens.

**Spelling.** The spelling checker's dictionaries are downloaded once, for your language, from
the dictionary server used by the Chromium browser engine inside the app. No text of yours is
sent for spell checking.

**Opening links.** Links you click in the app (a provider's key page, say) open in your
browser.

## Children

Scivelle is not directed to children and collects no personal information from anyone.
