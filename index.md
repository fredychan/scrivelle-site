# Scrivelle

A word processor for novelists on the Mac: Word documents on real pages, your cast and your
notes beside the text, and a writing assistant on the page — Apple's own on-device model, or
the AI service you already use.

**Support.** This page is the User Guide, with every feature explained. Questions, problems and
ideas: write to [gh0stzz83@gmail.com](mailto:gh0stzz83@gmail.com). The [privacy policy](privacy)
says what the app does with your writing (in short: nothing leaves your Mac unless you connect
an AI service and ask it something).

---

Scrivelle is a word processor for novelists on the Mac. It opens and saves Word documents,
lays your book out on real pages as you write, keeps the cast and the world of your story
beside the text, and brings a writing assistant to the page — Apple's own on-device model, or
the AI service you already pay for.

This guide covers everything in the app. The first three sections are enough to start writing.

---

## 1. Getting started

**Open the app.** A new, empty document is waiting: a page in the middle, the ribbon above it,
the status bar below. Start typing.

**The window, top to bottom.**

- **Title bar** — the document's name and the AutoSave switch. With AutoSave on, the document is
  saved a moment after every change; you never need to remember to save.
- **Ribbon** — the tabs Home, Insert, Layout, Review and Assistant. Tabs for pictures, shapes,
  text boxes and tables appear when one is selected. The chevron at the right collapses the
  ribbon to its tab names.
- **The page** — your book, on pages the size you choose, with a running head and page numbers.
- **Manuscript pane** (left, when open) — chapters and scenes, the cast, your notes and Ask.
- **Assistant pane** (right, when open) — where the writing assistant answers.
- **Status bar** — the page you are looking at, the word count, reading time, zoom and the layout
  mode. Click the word count for a full count including the selection.

**Your first document.** Home ▸ New starts a fresh document, Home ▸ Open opens one, and Home ▸
Recent lists what you worked on lately. ⌘S saves; the first time, you choose the name and the
format.

**Formats.** Scrivelle reads and writes Word documents (`.docx`), its own web documents (`.html`),
Markdown (`.md`) and plain text (`.txt`). Word is the format to use with editors, agents and
other word processors: formatting, styles, comments, tracked changes, pictures, the cast and
your notes all travel in it.

## 2. Writing

**Text.** The Home tab has the font, size, bold, italic, underline, colour and highlight, the
paragraph alignment, lists and indents. Double-click a word to select it; triple-click selects a
paragraph. The usual shortcuts work: ⌘B, ⌘I, ⌘U, ⌘Z to undo, ⇧⌘Z to redo.

**Styles.** The Styles gallery on the Home tab holds Normal, the headings, Title, Subtitle and
any styles of your own. A style sets the look of a whole paragraph at once, and changing a style
changes every paragraph that uses it. Right-click a style for *Modify Style…*; *New Style…*
makes one from the paragraph at the caret. Heading 1 is a chapter and Heading 2 a scene (see
*Chapters*). Styles live in the document and go into `.docx` as Word styles.

**Paragraphs.** Home ▸ Paragraph (the small arrow in the group's corner) sets indents, spacing,
line height and the *Line and Page Breaks* rules — keep with next, keep lines together, page
break before, widow and orphan control — which the pages honour on screen and in print.

**Spelling.** Misspelled words get a red underline. Right-click one for suggestions and *Learn
Spelling*. This works on the page and in every text field of the Manuscript pane.

**Find and replace.** ⌘F opens Find, ⇧⌘H Replace. Matches are highlighted on the page; Enter
goes to the next.

**Scene breaks.** Manuscript ▸ *Scene break* inserts a centred `* * *`.

**Clear formatting** (Home) returns the selection to the paragraph's style.

---

## 3. Pages

**Page setup.** The Layout tab sets the paper size (Letter, A4 and more), the orientation and
the margins. The page on screen is the page that prints.

**Headers, footers and page numbers.** Insert ▸ Header and Insert ▸ Footer set text that repeats
on every page. Insert ▸ Page Number puts "Page 3" bottom right (where a new document starts
out), bottom centre, top right, or nowhere.

**Chapter titles in the running head.** New documents show each chapter's title at the top of
every page of that chapter, as a ruled line — "CHAPTER 3 · FIRST ACQUAINTANCES" — instead of
as a heading in the text. See *Chapters* for how to turn this off and on.

**Layout modes.** The status bar switches between *Print Layout* (pages, the default) and *Web
Layout* (one continuous sheet). Zoom with the slider, ⌘+ and ⌘−, or pinch on the trackpad.

**Printing and PDF.** File ▸ Print prints the pages you see, headers and numbers included. File
▸ Export to PDF writes the same pages to a PDF.

---

## 4. Pictures, shapes, text boxes and tables

The Insert tab adds **pictures** from a file (or paste or drop one), **shapes**, **text boxes**
and **tables**. Select one and its own tab appears: wrap the text around it or run the text
over it, size it, rotate it, position it on the page, group several, give a table borders and
shading, merge cells. Pictures and shapes go into `.docx` as Word objects.

---

## 5. Reviewing

**Track changes.** Review ▸ Track Changes records every insertion, deletion and formatting
change with your name and the time. *Accept* and *Reject* work on the change at the caret or on
all of them; *Previous* and *Next* step through them. The markup can be shown in full, as a
simple view, or hidden. Changes travel in `.docx` as Word revisions, so an editor using Word
sees them, and theirs come back to you.

**Comments.** Review ▸ Comment (⌥⌘A) attaches a comment to the selection. Comments sit in the
margin beside their text; reply to one, resolve it, or delete it. They travel in `.docx` too.

**Your name.** Comments and changes carry the name of your macOS account.

## 6. Chapters and the Manuscript pane

Home ▸ Manuscript (⌥⌘M) opens the Manuscript pane. It has four tabs.

### Outline

Your book's structure: chapters are Heading 1 paragraphs, scenes Heading 2, each with its word
count. The pane counts your words today against a daily goal you set, per document, starting
afresh each day.

- **+ Chapter** starts a new chapter after the one you are in, on a new page, numbered. The
  first chapter of an empty document starts on page 1.
- **+ Scene** starts a scene; **Scene break** inserts `* * *`.
- **Chapter entries** show the chapter's number and its title in a field. Type the title there:
  the heading in the book follows. Click an entry and the chapter's page comes to the top of the
  window, with the caret in it. Drag a chapter onto another to move it, text and all; drop it on
  the last chapter to put it at the end. ⌘Z undoes a move.
- **Title in header** decides where chapter titles live. On (the default), every page of a
  chapter carries the title as a ruled line in the running head, and the heading takes no room on
  the page; you edit the title in the pane. Off, the title is a heading on the page, as in Word.
  Headings that already name themselves — "Chapter 3", "Prologue" — are shown as they are, and a
  prologue or epilogue takes no number.
- **Sticky notes.** The pin at the right of a chapter or scene opens a note to yourself about
  that part. A filled pin means there is one.
- **Plot grid** lays your plot lines against the chapters and scenes: a row for each line, a
  cell for what each part does for it.

### Cast

A profile for everyone in the story: name, the other names the text uses for them, their role,
and your notes. Each character is traced through the manuscript — every mention, grouped by
chapter with its sentence, a click away. *Tell their history* has the assistant read those
passages and tell what happens to the character, chapter by chapter, from the text alone.
*Generate* invents a new character from a brief, in the world of your story, as a sheet you can
add to the cast with one click. The cast is saved inside the document.

### Notes

World notes — places, customs, lore, anything the story needs remembered — as titled pages,
saved with the document. *Insert* puts a note into the manuscript at the caret.

**Series.** Books in a series share their cast and world notes through a series file. *New
series…* creates one next to the book; *Join series…* links a book to an existing one. Mark a
character or a note as shared and every book in the series has it.

### Ask

A research companion: ask what the Arctic looks like at night, how the first people made fire,
what a Victorian letter sounded like, and put the answer into the manuscript with *Insert*.
When the assistant reads the whole book (see below), Ask also answers questions about your own
story — who promised what, where things stand — from the text, naming the chapter.

## 7. The writing assistant

The Assistant tab puts the assistant to your text. Select a passage, or just leave the caret in
a paragraph, and choose:

- **Brainstorm** — five ideas for what could happen next, in your story's genre.
- **Continue Writing** — the next paragraphs, in your voice.
- **Suggest Titles** — titles for the chapter or the book.
- **Rewrite** — the passage, polished in its own tone or in one you pick: horror, suspense,
  romantic, humorous, literary, plain, formal, concise, vivid.
- **Proofread** — spelling, grammar and punctuation, nothing else.
- **Summarize** — a synopsis.
- **Synonyms** — alternatives for a word or phrase, each a chip that drops in with a click.
- **Ask a Question** and **Create a Character** open the Manuscript pane's Ask and Cast.
- **Read Aloud** reads the selection in the system voice.

The answer streams into the assistant pane. Nothing touches your manuscript until you choose
**Replace**, **Insert** or **Apply as tracked changes** — the last turns a rewrite or a
proofread into insertions and deletions you accept or reject one by one. Right-click a
selection for *Rewrite with Assistant* and *Proofread Selection*. *Stop* stops a request.

### Who answers

**Apple Intelligence**, on this Mac, is the default: nothing leaves your computer, there is no
account and no key. It needs macOS 26 or later on an Apple silicon Mac with Apple Intelligence
turned on in System Settings. It reads about 1,400 words at a time and writes short passages.

**Your own AI.** Assistant ▸ AI Settings (or Settings ▸ Writing assistant) lets you choose
instead:

- **ChatGPT** — *Continue with ChatGPT* signs you in in your browser, and your ChatGPT plan pays
  for the answers; or paste a key from OpenAI.
- **Claude** — paste a key from Anthropic. A Claude subscription cannot be used in other apps.
- **Gemini** — paste a key from Google AI Studio, which has a free tier.
- **OpenRouter** — one account for Claude, GPT, Gemini and many more: sign in, or paste a key.
- **Ollama** — free, open models that run on this Mac. Install Ollama, and Scrivelle finds it.
- **Other server** — any OpenAI-compatible server, by its address.

Each card walks you through it: a key is three steps — open the key page, create and copy a key,
paste it — and Scrivelle checks the connection before keeping it. The models on offer appear in
a menu with the best one for writing first. Keys and sign-ins are kept on this Mac, in the app's
own folder, and only ever sent to the provider they belong to. The assistant pane's footer always
says which engine is answering and where your text goes.

### What the assistant reads

Also in AI Settings:

- **Around the caret** (the default) — the passage and about 1,400 words of the text. Fast, cheap,
  and all Apple Intelligence or a local model can take.
- **The whole book** — with a connected provider, every request carries your manuscript (up to
  80,000 words). A continuation in chapter twelve knows chapters one to eleven; a rewrite keeps
  names and facts straight; a new character is invented against the whole cast; a character's
  history draws on every mention; and Ask answers questions about the story itself. Each request
  takes longer and costs more — a 90,000-word novel is roughly 30 cents on a Claude key, or a
  bite of a ChatGPT plan. Apple Intelligence and Ollama keep reading around the caret.

## 8. Files, versions and recovery

- **AutoSave** (title bar) saves after every change. A document that has never been saved is
  asked for a name the first time.
- **Version History** (File menu) keeps a copy of the document at every save — every explicit
  save, and autosaves at least ten minutes apart, up to fifty — and restores any of them.
- **Recent** (Home tab) lists the documents opened or saved lately; *Clear Recent* empties it.
- **Markdown and plain text** keep the text and the basic formatting; use `.docx` to keep
  everything.
- **Cloud.** A document kept in iCloud Drive, Dropbox or OneDrive — and a series file beside it
  — syncs as any file does.

## 11. Tips and troubleshooting

- **"Apple Intelligence is not available."** Turn it on in System Settings ▸ Apple Intelligence
  & Siri (macOS 26 or later, Apple silicon), or connect a provider in AI Settings instead.
- **"Ollama isn't running."** Open the Ollama app, then *Check again* in AI Settings. If it has
  no models yet, run `ollama pull gemma3` in Terminal once.
- **A key is not accepted.** Keys are long and easy to cut short; copy it again from the
  provider's key page and paste it whole. A ChatGPT sign-in that has expired is renewed with
  *Continue with ChatGPT* again.
- **The page number in the status bar** follows the window as you scroll, and the caret when you
  click or type.
- **Words today** counts from the first time you open the document each calendar day; closing
  and reopening the app keeps the count.
- **Something went wrong in the text.** File ▸ Version History has a copy from every save.
- **A Word file looks odd.** Scrivelle ignores impossible values it meets in a file (an indent
  wider than the page, say); saving the file once writes it back clean.

---

## 12. Privacy, in short

Scrivelle keeps your documents wherever you save them and its settings on your Mac. It has no
account, collects nothing and sends nothing anywhere on its own. Text leaves your Mac only when
you run the assistant with a provider you connected, and then only to that provider, under its
terms. See the privacy policy for the details.
